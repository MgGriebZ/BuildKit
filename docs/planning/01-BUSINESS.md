# Business hypothesis: focused build sessions

Revision 0.5 · Proposed operating plan. Thresholds are pilot choices, not market benchmarks.

## Audience and promise

Start with adult learners who want seminar-led teaching about a small project. The 60-minute session teaches through visual explanation, a prepared working example, one focused learner-chosen refinement and practical next steps. The first example is a standalone, bedroom-inspired Cozy Room built with Blazor WebAssembly, C#, HTML, CSS and SVG. Baseline behavior: a lamp cycles two styles and persists selection. The taught refinement adds a third style and gentle feedback. Mouse, touch, keyboard and reduced-motion support are retained. The learner gets a public follow-along and, for a personalized seminar, an agreed Build Kit. This is teaching with a working example, not open-ended debugging.

The differentiator to test is guided scoping, hands-on progress, and a personalized handoff—not access to prompts alone. RoadScript, MgGriebZ, an event page, a toy, or a simple organizer may provide examples. Use only cleared material and fictional data in public demonstrations. Project features are not evidence of customer benefit or demand.

## Primary offer and boundaries

One-to-one seminar: 60 minutes; proposed pilot price $75 remains unapproved. The first topic is Cozy Room. Before payment, agree the learner's goal, refinement, prerequisites and handoff. Include relevant project files or links, prompts, what was verified and personal next steps. Custom seminar creation is a separate reusable preparation investment, recorded apart from delivery economics. Bound personalized extra preparation; re-scope and quote it before selling if it exceeds the included plan. Track prep and follow-up time. The standalone room and free public follow-along are in scope; paid standalone course/LMS is deferred. No new subscriptions or routine credit reports are assumed; credit use is not promised.

Possible outcomes include a scoped specification, a working small change, a basic event page, or an unblocked project milestone. Do not promise a complete toy or application in 60 minutes. Exclude production readiness, security/privacy audits, ongoing maintenance, open-ended debugging, and third-party account work unless separately scoped. Timebox the work and explain tradeoffs. Client owns their project and makes final decisions; do not place credentials or sensitive data in prompts or shared files.

No-fit/no-result proposal: screen project fit before accepting payment. If the request cannot be served, decline and refund any payment for an unstarted session. If the provider cannot deliver the agreed milestone, explain the blocker, hand over usable work completed, and let the client choose a full refund or a mutually agreed no-charge corrective follow-up/reschedule. A new paid quote is for additional scope, not a condition for receiving the originally promised result. Exact cancellation/no-show rules and a feasible corrective-follow-up cap remain owner choices; see [session preparation](09-SESSION-PREP.md).

Paid standalone self-serve course/LMS: deferred. Do not advertise a ~$50 value, “normally $50,” or comparable valuation. Reconsider only after repeated independent-learning demand and material passes its own completion and support gates. The public free Cozy Room follow-along is in scope and is distinct from a paid self-serve course.

Free one-hour community/public-event classes: a core early service and outreach activity. Share the prepared demonstration foundation with paid seminars, teach one understandable refinement, and provide a cleared public worksheet/demo takeaway useful without buying anything. Paid sessions add individual scoping, personalization and a tailored handoff; free attendees do not owe a sales conversation. A first free class can precede paid demand or a new website. Confirm host/audience and delivery readiness before committing to an event.

## Funnel and evidence

Use this sequence: inquiry → scope/fit → choose slot → pay → work together → handoff/follow-up. The owner confirms fit and the agreed outcome before offering a slot and payment link. Keep a minimal private ledger: anonymous lead ID, source, use case, scope/fit result, slot, paid/refunded status, delivery, time, outcome, support, and permission for any testimonial. Contact details stay outside the public repository.

Ask what the person was trying to make, where they got stuck, what they have tried, and what useful progress would look like. Then show the actual session scope and proposed price. Treat interest, free attendance, and compliments separately from payment and delivered outcomes.

| Review gate | Evidence and response |
|---|---|
| Fit | At least 5 relevant conversations; identify recurring needs and requests the session cannot serve. Refine scope before expanding. |
| First two paid sessions | Complete two paid, scoped sessions and record total owner time, actual handoff, client-reported ability to continue, and unresolved issues. Review price, scope, and support before pursuing volume. |
| Delivery quality | After each session, verify the agreed handoff exists, disclose what was and was not tested, and ask whether the next step is clear. Repair worksheet/material defects. |
| Community pilot | After the first free class, review what participants understood/could repeat, host feedback, total preparation/delivery time and voluntary inquiries separately. A useful class is a community outcome, not proof of paid demand. |
| Third paid-session review | After three delivered paid sessions, review repeated needs, total time, material usefulness and support burden; adjust the offer or test one extension. No automatic standalone course launch follows. |

If no one books, distinguish an incomplete test from a rejected offer: record the number of qualified inquiries/offers, objections, and fit. After 10 genuinely relevant offers without a booking, review the wording, audience, scope, and price before building more product. This threshold is a proposed decision aid, not a demand forecast.

## Economics and capacity

The $75/60-minute figure is a proposed pilot price only. Do not present a simplistic hourly return that hides custom seminar preparation. Record reusable seminar-prep separately from each learner's personalization, live time and handoff. No income or profit is forecast.

Stripe currently lists US domestic card pricing as 2.9% + $0.30; verify the actual account, payment method, and applicable costs before use ([Stripe pricing](https://stripe.com/us/pricing)). Fees and tax treatment vary. No sales volume, conversion rate, household contribution, tax conclusion, or legal conclusion is assumed here.

The owner intends to start with at least a 5–10-hour/week planning range and can expand as the business develops; this is not a five-hour cap or a fixed limit of two sessions. Actual booking windows, discretionary spending and required business setup remain unconfirmed. Before taking payment, resolve the relevant readiness items in `10-READINESS.md` and [05-OPERATIONS.md](05-OPERATIONS.md). Track reusable preparation, personalized prep, delivery, travel/setup, follow-up, acquisition, refunds and out-of-pocket costs separately. Allocate time to free classes deliberately; increase commitments based on real availability and delivery experience, without treating free attendance as revenue.

## Expansion conditions

Make the shared teaching core repeatable and both public and personalized handoffs useful. Create or deepen shared learner material in response to actual needs. Free classes are already in launch scope; later paid group workshops and custom engagements need their own scope and demand evidence. Revisit a standalone course only after repeated independent-learning demand and a completed, tested learner path. As interest grows, review which free events best serve the community and fit the calendar rather than promising unlimited availability. Do not infer paying demand from social reach alone.
