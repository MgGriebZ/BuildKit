# Human operations and business setup

Research checked September 27, 2026. Planning assumes Minnesota because the original PRD identifies Pipestone; confirm where the business is actually operated. This is a preparation checklist, not a determination of the owner's individual legal or tax obligations. No registrations, tax returns or account changes have been submitted.

## Before accepting money

1. Confirm the actual seller: legal name/entity, business location, public trading name, whether an EIN/DBA/entity already exists, and the correct payment-account holder. Do not put personal identifiers in this public repository. Reuse existing registrations only after confirming they apply; do not duplicate them by default.
2. Decide sole proprietorship versus entity with appropriate local advice based on the services, liability and existing situation. An LLC is neither automatically required to validate demand nor a substitute for contracts, insurance and sound operations. Resolve host/client insurance requirements before agreeing to those engagements.
3. Check the proposed trading name and required filings. Minnesota says businesses using a name other than the applicable true/legal name must file an assumed name; after filing, publication in a qualified legal newspaper for two consecutive issues is required. Confirm applicability, current fees and publication cost; retain the affidavit and track renewals. [Minnesota Secretary of State](https://www.sos.mn.gov/business-liens/business-forms-fees/assumed-namedba).
4. Determine whether an EIN is required or useful for banking/state purposes. Obtain one directly from the IRS if needed; the IRS issues EINs free. Existing sole proprietorships generally do not need a separate EIN for every trade name. [IRS EIN guidance](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/employer-identification-number), [SS-4 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/iss4).
5. Set up separate business records and a suitable account/payment destination. Record gross receipts, refunds, processing fees, expenses, tax collected and owner withdrawals separately. Confirm provider/bank identity verification. Reconcile monthly; test a payment and refund in sandbox first.
6. Resolve tax classification for each offer, register where required, and configure collection before taxable sales. Stripe payment processing or a tax-calculation setting is not evidence that all registration/remittance duties are handled. [Minnesota sales/use tax guide](https://www.revenue.state.mn.us/book/export/html/10021).
7. Approve and publish actual session scope, support, refund, cancellation, license and privacy terms matching delivery. Set a realistic handoff response window and calendar capacity. The current offers include a standalone introductory guide and optional live instruction. Specify the actual formats, edition, delivery, support and update terms for each. Advertise only available materials.

## Questions for Minnesota Revenue or an accountant

Revision 0.6 brings a standalone guide into the initial product scope. Describe downloadable written material, any recording, live interactive group/one-to-one instruction and custom development separately to the adviser. Ask how each actual offer or bundle is classified; calling materials "included" does not itself settle tax treatment. Ask about registration, sourcing, bundled charges, documentation of exemptions and filing frequency. Keep the answer and date in private business records.

The prior PRD's blanket statement about digital-course tax is too broad. Minnesota lists taxable digital books and audio/audiovisual products but also has conditions under which interactive webinars are exempt. Written materials, live sessions and recordings should not automatically receive one classification. Educational purpose alone does not establish an exemption for this business. [Minnesota digital-products guidance](https://www.revenue.state.mn.us/digital-products).

Also ask about out-of-state sales, marketplace arrangements and any existing nexus. Do not assume "small business" makes every sale exempt or that every state's rule is a single revenue threshold. Start with a known customer geography and record enough purchase information to apply the confirmed rules.

## Federal income-tax preparation

A sole proprietor generally reports business profit/loss on Schedule C, and self-employment tax may apply. Estimated payments depend on the household's actual tax situation; IRS guidance generally uses an expected balance of $1,000 or more for individuals, with withholding and other rules affecting the calculation. Use professional advice or the appropriate IRS calculation rather than an invented flat reserve percentage. Separate sales-tax liabilities from estimated income/self-employment tax reserves. [IRS sole proprietorships](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/sole-proprietorships), [Publication 583](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p583).

"Tax submitted" should mean a specific return/payment for a specific period with a retained confirmation, not merely getting an EIN. Keep a calendar of actual registration, filing and payment obligations once established.

## Policy decisions to turn into public terms

Proposed session terms are detailed in [the preparation runbook](09-SESSION-PREP.md): one reschedule with 24 hours' notice; full refund if the owner cancels; and a clear remedy if the agreed milestone cannot be delivered. Owner must choose cancellation/no-show treatment and support boundaries before sales. Standalone guide sales now need their own approved refund, delivery-failure, support and edition-update terms. The former 14-day proposal is not automatically adopted and must not be applied ambiguously to live sessions.

License: learner may use their own resulting project; define rights for original starter code, supplied art and included guide prose separately. No blanket promise about third-party materials. Proposed guide terms allow the client's own learning and use, without resale of the guide as a course. Confirm ownership before finalizing this wording.

Privacy: minimum necessary purchase/support data, no card storage in BuildKit, actual service providers listed, optional marketing consent, named contact and retention/deletion process. Recording/testimonial permission is separate from payment. Use fictional public demos; check workplace IP/confidentiality before showing work-related material.
